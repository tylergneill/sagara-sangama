सागरसङ्गमः — “meeting of oceans”

Sāgarasaṅgama is a virtual confluence point for large online Sanskrit e-text collections. Each component project gets its own Atlas interface, and Sāgarasaṅgama sums over them.

Atlas concept

An "atlas" is a navigational layer over a collection someone else hosts. It charts the navigable waters with improved navigation, and it plumbs their depths with sizes, counts, and growth dynamics that the source collection doesn't surface on its own. No Atlas hosts text content of its own, instead linking back to the source.

Why Atlases?

These large collections are poorly understood in their entireties because their own web interfaces are at once too bespoke and too limited. By standardizing navigation UX and giving more attention to issues of scope — the added info about sizes, counts, and growth dynamics — users will appreciate these collections at a deeper level and make better use of them day to day.

In addition, an Atlas surfaces structural problems in the source collection so that they can be fixed upstream.

Saṅgama concept

Any one Atlas can say how large its own collection is, but only the confluence can say how multiple compare, or answer the question a reader arrives with: who if anyone has this text? Sāgarasaṅgama searches titles and authors across all Atlases, charts their scope and growth against each other, and keeps a front door for collections most readers meet one at a time.

*While E-bhāratīsampat and Sanskrit Wikisource PDFs counts represent scans tied to their own texts and hosted on the site, Sanskrit Documents links e-texts to scans of editions only loosely, and the great majority are hosted not on the site itself, but elsewhere (chiefly archive.org). These outbound links are checked for liveness, and dead ones are not counted. Each unique PDF is counted only once (e.g., forty stotra documents may link to a single scan).
